Dashboard 1 - Driver Safety Scoring

Each driver gets a score from 0 to 100, where 100 means perfectly safe and lower scores mean riskier driving. The score comes entirely from the phone sensors recorded every minute during each trip, so it is objective with no guesswork or self-reporting.

Step 1 - Detect risky events

Each row in the sensor data is one minute of driving. We check four types of risky behaviour using simple cutoff values on the sensor readings:

Event Sensor Used Cutoff Why this cutoff?
Harsh Braking Accel_X_g below -0.4 g The X-axis sensor measures forward and backward force. A reading below -0.4g means the rider braked very suddenly. Normal firm braking is around -0.2g. Only 2.7% of all driving minutes cross this line, so it only catches real events.
Harsh Acceleration Accel_X_g above +0.4 g Same sensor, opposite direction. Above +0.4g means the rider hit the throttle aggressively. Only 3.0% of minutes are flagged.
Hard Cornering Gyro_Z_dps above 20 deg/sec Gyro_Z measures how fast the vehicle is turning. Normal turns are under 10 deg/sec. Above 20 is a sharp, aggressive turn that risks tipping on a two-wheeler. Only 3.8% of minutes are flagged.
Speeding Speed_kmph above 50 km/h The Bengaluru urban road speed limit is 50 km/h. Any minute above this is a traffic violation. Only 1.3% of minutes cross this, which makes sense for city delivery riding with an average speed of just 24 km/h.

Step 2 - Calculate a rate per driver

We count how many minutes each driver had each event, then divide by their total driving minutes. This gives a rate like "2 harsh brakes per 100 minutes". Without this step, a driver who did more trips would always look worse just because they drove more, which is unfair.

Step 3 - Turn the rate into a score (0 to 100)

We use a simple formula: the driver with the lowest rate in the fleet gets 100, the driver with the highest rate gets 0, and everyone else falls in between.

  • Score = (1 - (rate - lowest rate) / (highest rate - lowest rate)) x 100
  • This means the scoring is relative to this fleet, not an external benchmark.

Step 4 - Combine into one final score

The four component scores are combined with weights that reflect how dangerous each behaviour is:

ComponentWeightWhy this weight?
Harsh Braking 30% Sudden braking is the most direct cause of rear-end collisions in delivery fleets. Highest weight.
Harsh Acceleration 25% Aggressive throttle puts stress on the engine and signals impulsive riding.
Hard Cornering 25% Sharp turns are a leading cause of two-wheeler tip-overs.
Speeding 20% Important, but drivers who speed also tend to brake harder, so this is partly captured by the other scores already. Slightly lower weight to avoid counting it twice.

Step 5 - Assign a label

ScoreLabelWhat to do
75 to 100SAFENo action needed. Recognise them.
50 to 74MONITORSchedule a coaching session. Watch for trends.
0 to 49HIGH RISKAct immediately. Retrain or reassign.

Assumptions

  • The phone is mounted in a fixed position so the sensor axis stays consistent.
  • All trips are urban delivery routes in Bengaluru, so 50 km/h applies.
  • Each sensor row is exactly 1 minute of driving.
  • The X-axis is the forward/backward direction (standard handlebar mount).

Dashboard 2 - Vehicle Health Scoring

Each vehicle gets a score from 0 to 100 where 100 means perfectly healthy. We combine live sensor signals with the vehicle records to judge mechanical condition.

Signal Where it comes from Weight What it tells us
Vibration Level Spread of vertical sensor readings (Accel_Z_g) 35% On a smooth, healthy vehicle the vertical sensor should stay close to 1.0g (gravity). If the reading jumps around a lot, the vehicle is vibrating more than it should. High vibration means worn bearings, loose parts or an unbalanced wheel. This is the strongest real-time signal of wear.
Sensor Spike Rate Percentage of minutes where Accel_Z_g is outside 0.7 to 1.3 30% On flat road at steady speed, the vertical reading should stay near 1.0g. Values outside the 0.7 to 1.3 range are unusual jolts. Some are just potholes, but a vehicle that consistently produces these readings likely has a suspension or frame issue.
Age and Mileage Year of manufacture and odometer reading 20% Parts wear with both time and use. We combine vehicle age and odometer reading into one wear number. A 2019 vehicle with 46,000 km is much more worn than a 2025 vehicle with 2,700 km.
Time Since Last Service Last_Service_Date 15% Regular servicing catches problems early. We count the days since the last service up to 6 August 2026. Vehicles not serviced in over 90 days are flagged.

Scoring

Same approach as driver scoring. Higher signal value means worse health, so we invert it to get a score where 100 is the healthiest.

Labels

ScoreLabelWhat to do
70 to 100HEALTHYKeep up with the normal service schedule.
45 to 69MONITORBook a service within 2 weeks.
0 to 44URGENTTake the vehicle off the road for inspection now.

Assumptions

  • All vehicles operate on similar urban roads in Bengaluru.
  • The Z-axis sensor is vertical (pointing up through the vehicle).
  • The reference date for the service gap is 6 August 2026, the last day in the dataset.
  • The odometer reading is from the start of the week, so it slightly underestimates total wear.

Other Ways to Use This Data

Beyond these two dashboards, the same dataset could power:

Use CaseHow
Better Route Planning GPS coordinates and trip durations show which routes have higher average speeds, meaning less traffic. This could feed into a smarter routing tool.
Insurance Risk Scoring The driver safety score is essentially what insurance companies call a telematics score. Risky drivers could be flagged for higher premiums or targeted training.
Predicting Maintenance With more historical data, rising vibration trends over time could warn you before a vehicle breaks down, so you can service it proactively.
Targeted Driver Training The event breakdown tells you exactly what to train. A driver with lots of cornering events needs different coaching from one who mostly speeds.
Hub Performance Drivers belong to home hubs. Averaging scores by hub shows whether certain locations have worse drivers, pointing to local training or road issues.
Night Driving Risk Trip timestamps let you identify night trips (after 8pm). If night trips have higher incident rates, you could adjust shift schedules to reduce risk.
